Town context, method and sources
How to read the map
  • Fuji view: clear means nothing hides the mountain. Partly hidden means the lower part is covered. Hidden means only the tip, or nothing.
  • The view check uses the land, the forests and the buildings. Forest is counted as 15 m tall. Single trees and hedges are not in it.
  • Neighbours counts the buildings within 100 m of the spot.
  • Land you could live on leaves out forest, water, roads, slopes steeper than 15 degrees and land with no land-use record. It is not a permission. Check the zone of the spot.
  • Rare spots ranks land you could live on, one quality at a time: Fuji view, distance to the nearest building, buildings within 100 m, height, flat ground, and the age of nearby houses. A spot has a quality when it is in the top 5, 10 or 20% for it. You choose which.
  • The colour says how many separate places in the town share all of a spot's qualities. A place is a group of touching squares. Purple at its darkest means this is the only one.
  • Rare does not mean good. It means unusual here. The tapped spot says which qualities it has.
  • Uses tests three uses against each spot: a noisy workshop, a garden with trees, a small guesthouse. Each need is met, fails, needs a check, or is not known. Not known never counts as met.
  • Rules of law are always marked "check this". The page names the law. It is not legal advice.
  • Noise: the nearest building must be far enough for the sound to fall to 55 dB, the daytime standard where people mainly live. Sound falls by 6 dB each time the distance doubles. You set how loud the work is.
  • Sun: hours of direct sun on the shortest day, between 8:00 and 16:00 solar time, past land, forest and buildings. You set how many hours the garden needs.
  • Past prices use reported transactions for land of at least 150 m². The range is the middle half of sales, not an estimate for one property. Fewer than five sales is too little to show a range.
  • Spots within 6 km of the summit count as the mountain itself. Eye height is 1.6 m. One square is about 31 m wide.

Sources: Geospatial Information Authority of Japan elevation tiles. National Land Numerical Information town boundary, railways and riders per station (2024). MLIT Real Estate Information Library transaction prices (2021–2026).
