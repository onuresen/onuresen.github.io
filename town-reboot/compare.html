How to read it

  • Every 31 m square is tested. The table adds up the squares that pass every condition you picked.
  • A purpose is only a starting point. It switches the visible land needs. Change any need to make your own version.
  • "Selected data complete" means this town's model contains every layer needed by the selected conditions. It does not mean the place is approved, safe or suitable.
  • Land you could live on: slope under 15°, not forest, water or road. Where a town's 3D model has no land-use record, only slope and lakes are used, so more land counts.
  • "Not known" means the town's 3D model does not have that layer. It is never counted as a pass.
  • Clear view of Mt. Fuji: nothing hides the mountain, past land, forest and buildings. From far towns the mountain is small even when clear.
  • From your home: straight-line km, and drive time without traffic where the page can reach the OpenStreetMap routing server (the public site). Set your home on a town map or the finder; it is saved only in this browser.
  • By train: the fastest way by local trains, from your home or Kawasaki Station, to the town's best station. Estimated from the national rail network (track length, stops, 6 minutes per change). It does not know timetables or express trains. "km out" means the town has no station; the nearest one is that far from its centre.
  • Past prices use reported transactions for land of at least 150 m². Each range is the middle half of sales, not an estimate for one property.
  • The budget limit keeps a town when the low end of that range is within budget. It does not promise a property is available at that price.
  • What changes if compares exact town sets against your starting point. A town is possible when it is within the price and travel limits, has the selected evidence, and has some tested land meeting every selected need.
  • Biggest opening tests each available one-step trade-off. It reports added towns first, then added matching land. It is not a score or recommendation.
  • These are areas, not a ranking of towns. Open a town's map to see where the land is.
