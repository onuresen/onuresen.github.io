What newcomers are rarely told about contributing in Japan.

Money · Japan tax basics

A donation may reduce your Japanese tax. It is not automatic.

It depends on the recipient's legal status, your tax situation, the donation, and sometimes local rules. A well-known name or an NPO label does not guarantee a deduction.

Keep the official receipt 領収書 / 寄附金受領証明書

It should show your name, the recipient, the amount and the date. Keep any certificate the organization sends.

  1. Check the recipient

    Look for 認定NPO法人, 公益社団法人 or 公益財団法人. Confirm that your specific donation qualifies.

  2. Choose the treatment

    For qualifying donations to certified NPOs, individuals may be able to choose an income deduction or a tax credit. The better choice varies.

  3. File if required

    Enter eligible donations in your income-tax return (確定申告). Keep or submit the evidence.

  4. Check resident tax too

    Local tax relief can depend on whether your prefecture or city designates the recipient.

Charitable donation

NGO, certified NPO or public-interest body

Your money supports the organization's work. Tax treatment depends on the recipient and the donation. Ask for the official receipt.

ふるさと納税

Donation to a municipality

A different system with its own limits and a one-stop procedure. If you later file a tax return, the one-stop applications stop counting. You must then include all furusato donations in the return.

This is orientation, not tax advice

Rules, limits and your residency status matter. Confirm with the organization, the National Tax Agency, your city office or a tax professional.

About

What One Sekai is

A small guide for international residents and visitors who want to contribute in Japan and do not know where to start.

What it does

  • Lists a few organizations as starting points.
  • Says what each one asks for: language, time, place.
  • Links to the organization's own pages.
  • Explains the basics newcomers often miss.

What it is not

  • Not an NPO and not a volunteer coordinator.
  • Not a partner of any organization listed.
  • Not an endorsement. Being listed is not a rating.
  • Not tax, visa or legal advice.

What “checked” means

  • We read the organization's official page on the date shown.
  • We wrote down what that page said.
  • We did not assess its safety, finances or operations.
  • Openings and rules change. The organizer decides who can join.

How entries are written

  • Official pages only. No copied application forms.
  • An English page does not mean every role works in English. We say which.
  • We say when an organization mainly takes donations, or when Japanese is needed.
  • Work with children, vulnerable people, animals and disaster survivors stays under the organizer's screening.
